# S1-06.6 Separation-of-Duties Approval Channel Blocker

## Status

`Blocked`. The acceptance criteria require an authenticated, protected-control-plane
separation-of-duties decision that can be verified at the owning Gate. The current
repository deliberately has no such decision channel. Implementing an application
role check, an approval endpoint, or a standalone promotion/reconciliation service
would create an untrusted authority surface and violate ADR-018.

This record does not add a role-to-operation mapping, approval authority,
persistence, a protected-state writer, a retrieval release, a promotion path, an
incident resolver, an Action runtime, or a claim that any target capability is
implemented.

Automated lifecycle handling cannot act as human incident resolver.

## Acceptance criteria and result

| Acceptance criterion | Result | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sensitive candidate producer cannot be the sole promoter. | Blocked | Candidate promotion is not implemented, and no Gate-owned authenticated role/independence decision exists. |
| A retrieval consumer cannot bypass source and policy assessment. | Blocked | Current MS-1 reads are not the MS-2 retrieval lifecycle; no source-assessment or retrieval-policy Gate exists. |
| Automated lifecycle handling cannot act as the human incident resolver. | Blocked | No authenticated human incident-resolution channel, authoritative incident state, or Gate-owned reconciliation contract exists. |
| Planner, executor, approver, and verifier cannot self-approve or self-verify where independence is required. | Blocked | Planner, executor, external-effect, and independent-verifier runtime capabilities are target-only before NB-5; no authenticated service-identity separation contract binds them at a Gate. |

## Required prerequisite: S1-06.6a Gate-owned separation-decision contract

Owner: `Neural Brain Protected Control Plane / Identity, Policy, and Gate Owners`.

Before S1-06.6 implementation may begin, an accepted, versioned contract must
define all of the following:

1. A protected, authenticated resolver for active Principal, Service Identity,
   scoped role binding, operation, policy decision, and evaluation/incident
   authority. It must derive facts from the authenticated runtime context and
   protected catalog only; request payloads, model output, and approval evidence
   cannot supply or widen them.
2. A typed separation decision for each applicable lifecycle: candidate
   producer/promoter, retrieval consumer/source-policy assessor, automatic
   reconciler/human incident resolver, planner/executor/approver/independent
   verifier. It must identify the relevant principals or service identities,
   scopes, operation, policy digest, expiry, and denial reason.
3. The specific owning Gate operations and durable state machines that receive
   the decision, re-resolve it at commit, and atomically persist the protected
   transition, separation receipt, and audit event. A general service or
   standalone approval repository is prohibited.
4. Explicit conditions for when identity distinction is insufficient, including
   required independence of service component, human reviewer, evaluation
   owner, incident authority, and policy author. Unknown or overlapping roles,
   stale bindings, unavailable identity sources, and ambiguous outcomes must
   deny or become `indeterminate`.
5. Database roles, RLS/FORCE policies, direct-write denials, recovery ownership,
   and Human Gate escalation rules. Automatic reconciliation may collect
   evidence but cannot resolve a human incident or release a disputed effect.

Expected follow-on artifacts after acceptance:

- ordered PostgreSQL migrations for authenticated role-resolution and each
  relevant Gate-owned receipt/lifecycle, only after its owning Gate contract;
- explicit Python ports/adapters that call the Gate and accept no request-supplied
  role or independence assertion;
- machine-readable separation and incident/reconciliation contracts plus
  operator runbooks;
- traceability from every separation invariant to migration, code, adversarial
  PostgreSQL test, recovery test, and independent review evidence.

## Required verification before completion

The eventual implementation must prove, against an isolated PostgreSQL 18
ledger and the applicable Gate, at minimum:

1. producer equals promoter, requester equals approver, planner equals executor,
   executor equals verifier, consumer equals required assessor, or automatic
   reconciler equals human resolver each denies when the operation requires
   independence;
2. an identity or role binding that is expired, revoked, cross-scope, wrong
   component, missing, forged in payload, or changed after preflight is denied
   at commit;
3. a valid independent decision is bound to exact scope, operation, policy,
   authority, resource, checkpoint/version, and expiry, then atomically auditable
   with the Gate transition;
4. concurrent attempts, crashes before and after commit, replay, stale fences,
   audit failure, and indeterminate outcomes leave no bypass and reconcile only
   from the authoritative receipt;
5. direct table writes, non-Gate database roles, general application roles, and
   Cognitive Plane components cannot create separation evidence, promote a
   candidate, expose a gated retrieval result, resolve an incident, execute an
   effect, or mark a goal achieved.

## Unblock condition

S1-06.6 is executable only after S1-06.6a is accepted and names the concrete
authenticated role-resolution source, operating scopes, Gate owners, durable
receipts, human incident authority, recovery semantics, database roles, and
test fixture. Each lifecycle may then be implemented only in its permitted
delivery stage; no S1-06.6 artifact advances NB-5, NB-6, or a Memory Core stage.
