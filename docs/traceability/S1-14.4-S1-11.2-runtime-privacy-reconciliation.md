## Missing Runtime Contract

The accepted runtime contract must eventually bind, for one exact artifact and
transaction:

1. trusted actor and immutable Tenant/Area/Project/Session scope;
2. deployment, environment, jurisdiction, controller/processor roles, and
   processing activity;
3. orthogonal personal-data, Article 9, Article 10, minor-data,
   pseudonymization, anonymization, and conflict status;
4. versioned purpose and processing-activity identifiers;
5. a qualified Article 6 resolution and any required additional Article 9 or
   Article 10 resolution;
6. safeguards, data minimization, subject/source provenance, retention, legal
   hold, deletion responsibility, and derivative handling;
7. immutable policy, evidence, classification, authority, approval, and
   activation digests with validity and revocation fences;
8. a deterministic decision and stable reason codes;
9. protected mutation, governance binding, receipt, and non-reconstructive
   audit evidence in one transaction.

Only `ALLOW` may permit a protected mutation. `DENY`,
`REQUIRE_HUMAN_REVIEW`, `REQUIRE_ADDITIONAL_EVIDENCE`, `EXPIRED`, `REVOKED`,
`CONFLICT`, and `UNKNOWN` must produce no protected data mutation.

## Non-Compensatory Blockers

The following cannot be compensated by any other positive signal:

- unknown or conflicting data classification;
- missing purpose or processing activity;
- missing Article 6 resolution;
- missing required Article 9 or Article 10 resolution;
- missing safeguard, retention, approval, owner, auditability, or deletion
  responsibility;
- unsupported or unknown jurisdiction;
- expired, revoked, stale, scope-mismatched, or digest-mismatched evidence;
- unresolved policy conflict or unknown system state;
- missing qualified privacy/legal review or independent activation evidence.

## Smallest Safe Preparation Slice

Before external authorization, repository work is limited to:

- a `Proposed` and non-authorizing ADR;
- machine-readable contract and strict JSON instance schemas;
- typed immutable metadata and lifecycle models;
- a preparation evaluator that can never emit `ALLOW`;
- a state machine whose activation transition is unavailable;
- schema and cutover proposals that create no active policy and grant no
  productive mutation authority;
- negative, property, and state-machine tests proving incomplete, unknown,
  expired, revoked, conflicting, or unapproved inputs never authorize;
- threat model, privacy-engineering requirements, runbooks, test strategy,
  decision package, traceability, and learning material.

The first post-authorization implementation slice must add protected policy,
evidence, approval, lifecycle, decision, and governance-binding persistence;
then atomically integrate transaction-current decision evaluation into the
Memory Transition Gate. It must not rely on an application-layer preflight.
